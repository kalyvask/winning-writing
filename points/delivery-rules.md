# Spoken delivery rules

Distilled from Allison Kluger and Burt Alper's *Strategic Communication* (GSBGEN 315/515), Stanford GSB. [speech-rules.md](speech-rules.md) covers how to *write* a talk; this file covers how to *deliver* one — and how to answer questions when the talk ends. Most credibility is won or lost here, in mechanics the speaker can't hear in themselves.

## The three Vs

A message lands through three channels at once. When they disagree, the audience believes the non-verbal ones.

- **Verbal — what you say.** First words set the tone; final words set the memory. Choose both deliberately. Everything in between: consistent, efficient, relevant to the listener ("what's in it for you"), no undefined jargon.
- **Vocal — how you say it.** Five dials:
  - **Pace** — slower than you think. What feels sluggish to you reads as composed to the room.
  - **Volume** — louder than you think. The back row sets the floor.
  - **Clarity** — enunciate proper nouns and technical terms; they're the words the audience can't guess from context.
  - **Tone** — speak with conviction. A correct sentence delivered tentatively reads as a guess.
  - **Fillers** — silence is golden. A pause reads as thought; "um" reads as doubt.
- **Visual — what they see.** Posture conveys confidence even seated. Gestures aligned with content make it land; gestures fighting the content erase it. Sustained eye contact connects; scanning reads as evasion.

(The oft-quoted 7/38/55 split is disproved as stated — but the ranking holds. The words carry less than the delivery.)

## The four derailers

Small, repeated missteps that compound into a credibility tax. Even accomplished speakers have them; none of them can hear their own. Each has a specific fix:

| Derailer | What it does | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Filler words** ("um," "uh," "like," "basically") | Signals uncertainty, invites tune-out | The **tactical pause** — replace the filler with silence. Practice by recording yourself and counting. |
| **Upward inflection** on statements | Turns assertions into questions; undermines authority | Start the sentence high, **end low**. Retrain the ear by listening for it in others first. |
| **Extraneous movement** — swaying, weight-shifting, fidgeting | Broadcasts nervousness | Plant the feet. Move only on purpose (a step to mark a transition). |
| **Self-commenting** — apologizing, narrating your own slips | Announces mistakes the audience never noticed | Correct smoothly and continue. **The audience does not know you made a mistake unless you tell them.** |

The practice loop for all four: **record and review**. You cannot fix a habit you cannot perceive; video closes the gap between what feels natural and what reads well. What feels good to you doesn't always make the best impression — and improvement passes through a stage where the fixed version feels worse than the habit.

## Posture

- Don't: cross your feet, shield your torso with your arms or notes, lean on furniture, make yourself small.
- Do: open stance, weight even, energy visible. Purposeful movement only.

## Answering questions

Q&A is where prepared speakers lose unprepared. Three structures, all short enough to hold under pressure:

- **Problem / Solution / Result** — for "how did you handle X?"
- **What / So What / Now What** — for "what's the status?"
- **Point / Reason / Example** — for opinion questions.

The rule above all three: **lead with the answer.** Then support it. A question answered in the last sentence of a two-minute response wasn't answered. If you don't know: say so, say how you'll find out, and stop — a confident "I don't know, I'll have it to you Friday" beats three sentences of improvised hedging.

## Nerves

- Reframe the symptom: the physiology of anxiety and excitement is the same — label it excitement.
- Don't borrow worry from outcomes that haven't happened.
- Breathe before you're introduced, not after.
- Find the friendly faces first and return to them.
- The audience is rooting for you. They want the talk to be good; they're not auditing it.

## The delivery checklist

- [ ] **Recorded a practice run** and watched it — fillers counted, inflections marked.
- [ ] **First sentence and final sentence** chosen deliberately (tone; memory).
- [ ] **Pace and volume** one notch beyond comfortable — slower, louder.
- [ ] **Feet planted**, stance open, movement only on transitions.
- [ ] **Q&A structures rehearsed** for the three questions you most expect — answer first, support after.
- [ ] **The question you don't want asked** has a one-sentence honest answer ready.
- [ ] **No apologies planned** — not for preparation, time, slides, or nerves.

## The test

Watch your recording with the sound off: would a stranger say this person is confident? Then listen with the screen off: does every sentence end lower than it began? Pass both and the words get their chance.
